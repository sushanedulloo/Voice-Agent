# ADR-001 — Cascaded speech pipeline, not speech-to-speech

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Accepted |
| **Date** | 15 September 2026 |
| **Deciders** | TransOrg engineering |
| **Supersedes** | — |

## Context

Two architectures are available for a real-time voice agent.

**Cascaded.** Audio → speech-to-text → reasoning over text → text-to-speech → audio. Each stage is
a separate, inspectable component.

**Speech-to-speech.** A single model maps audio directly to audio with no intermediate text.
Materially lower latency. Gnani.ai — our strongest technical competitor — ships a 5B-parameter
speech-to-speech model claiming sub-200 ms P95, against the ~900 ms a cascaded pipeline achieves.

We are losing this comparison on latency and will continue to.

## Decision

**Use a cascaded pipeline.** Accept the latency disadvantage deliberately.

## Rationale

Three reasons, all of which are compliance rather than engineering.

1. **The script must stay BLC-approved.** A cascaded pipeline lets the script spine be fixed text
   or pre-rendered audio, with the model choosing *which* approved line plays. A speech-to-speech
   model generates the audio itself — there is no point at which an approved string exists to
   compare against. See ADR-002; this decision is what makes ADR-002 possible.

2. **We owe a three-year transcript archive and a speech-to-text quality audit.** Both are named
   deliverables in SBI Card's charter (`CH-S4` features 5 and 6). A cascaded pipeline produces the
   transcript as a by-product of operating. Speech-to-speech makes it a bolt-on that must be
   generated by a second system, which then needs its own accuracy audit.

3. **Indic support in end-to-end speech models is materially weaker** than in dedicated Indic
   speech models — with the notable exception of Gnani's own, which we cannot use.

A fourth reason emerged from regulatory research: under TCCCPR, commercial voice scripts are
registered as DLT templates by the Principal Entity. A model that generates novel audio emits
unregistered script content on every call (see R-15). Cascaded architecture keeps every utterance
traceable to a registered template.

## Consequences

**Accepted costs.**
- Turn latency ~900 ms against a competitor's claimed sub-200 ms. We will not win a latency
  comparison and **must stop presenting latency as a strength** — our current internal deck does,
  and it is a losing argument.
- More moving parts: three models in the path instead of one.

**Benefits.**
- Script adherence is provable from logs alone, without listening to audio (NFR-303).
- The transcript archive and STT quality audit fall out of normal operation.
- Each stage is independently swappable — important given open item OI-5 and the possibility that
  InfoSec forces self-hosting (R-05).
- Pre-rendered audio becomes possible, which is worth ~₹0.18 per conversation (ADR-005).

**Mitigation for the latency cost.** Most turns play cached audio with no synthesis in the path,
which removes the TTS stage from the critical path for the majority of turns. Budget and
measurement are in `docs/DESIGN.md`.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Speech-to-speech end to end | No approved-string checkpoint; transcript archive becomes a bolt-on; weaker Indic coverage. The latency win does not buy anything the client is paying for. |
| Hybrid — S2S for chitchat, cascaded for script | Two behavioural regimes in one call is harder to audit than either alone, and the boundary is exactly where compliance risk concentrates. |

## Notes

This decision is a deliberate trade, not an oversight. If SBI Card's evaluation turns into a
latency bake-off we lose it — which is why the engagement strategy competes on cost per booked SR
and targeting, not on speech. See `Context.md` §8.5.
